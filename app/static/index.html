Indicator glossary — what the numbers mean

EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
Gives more weight to recent prices. Price above a rising EMA, or a faster EMA above a slower EMA, can support a bullish trend; the reverse can support a bearish view.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Measures recent momentum from 0 to 100. High readings can mean strong momentum or an overextended move; low readings can mean weak momentum or oversold conditions. It is not a buy/sell signal by itself.

MACD
Compares moving averages to show momentum direction and changes. A rising/positive reading can support LONG; falling/negative momentum can support SHORT.

ATR (Average True Range)
Measures typical price movement, not direction. It helps estimate volatility and set stops/targets with room for normal fluctuations.

Volume vs average
Compares current volume with its recent average. Higher volume can add conviction to a breakout or trend; low volume can make a move less convincing.

Support and resistance
Price areas where buying or selling has previously appeared. They can act as potential turning points or breakout levels, but they are not guaranteed barriers.

LONG / SHORT score
A count/weighting of the strategy rules favouring each direction. It is not a long/short market ratio, a probability, or a forecast of success.

Risk/reward (R:R) and higher-timeframe confirmation
R:R compares potential reward with planned risk. Higher-timeframe confirmation checks whether the broader chart agrees with the shorter-term signal; disagreement can downgrade a signal to WAIT.

Important: LONG/SHORT are rule-based research signals, not calibrated probabilities or investment recommendations. Paper trading only. Prices depend on the configured data provider and may be delayed.
