Test the current rule set on historical candles.
How to read this backtest

Win rate: number of simulated trades with a positive R result divided by all simulated trades with an exit (including time exits), multiplied by 100. A positive R is a win; zero or negative R is not a win. The denominator therefore includes zero-R outcomes.

Expectancy: average R earned or lost per simulated trade. Net R: sum of all trade R results. Profit factor: gross winning R divided by the absolute gross losing R. Maximum drawdown: largest peak-to-trough decline in cumulative R.

Simulation method: signal calculated on a candle close; entry at the next candle open; stop distance is max(1.5 × ATR(14), 0.2% of entry); target is 2× the risk distance; maximum holding period is shown in the result. Stop is assumed first if stop and target are both touched in one candle. Spread, fees, slippage, taxes and short-borrow costs are not included.

This is a historical rule simulation, not a prediction. It is not proof of future performance and is not a calibrated probability.

Paper-trading performance

What paper-trading performance means

This panel summarises simulated positions automatically created when the analysis produces a LONG or SHORT signal. It does not place orders with a broker and does not represent your real account balance.

Win rate = profitable closed paper trades ÷ all closed paper trades × 100. Open trades are excluded until a stop or target is reached. Expectancy is average R per closed trade; profit factor compares total winning R with total losing R; net R is the cumulative result; maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in cumulative R.

In the current paper engine, target exits are +2R and stop exits are −1R. If both are touched within the same candle, the stop is assumed to have happened first. This conservative assumption may not match the true intrabar sequence.

Indicator glossary — what the numbers mean

EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
Gives more weight to recent prices. Price above a rising EMA, or a faster EMA above a slower EMA, can support a bullish trend; the reverse can support a bearish view.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Measures recent momentum from 0 to 100. High readings can mean strong momentum or an overextended move; low readings can mean weak momentum or oversold conditions. It is not a buy/sell signal by itself.

MACD
Compares moving averages to show momentum direction and changes. A rising/positive reading can support LONG; falling/negative momentum can support SHORT.

ATR (Average True Range)
Measures typical price movement, not direction. It helps estimate volatility and set stops/targets with room for normal fluctuations.

Volume vs average
Compares current volume with its recent average. Higher volume can add conviction to a breakout or trend; low volume can make a move less convincing.

Support and resistance
Price areas where buying or selling has previously appeared. They can act as potential turning points or breakout levels, but they are not guaranteed barriers.

LONG / SHORT score
A count/weighting of the strategy rules favouring each direction. It is not a long/short market ratio, a probability, or a forecast of success.

Risk/reward (R:R) and higher-timeframe confirmation
R:R compares potential reward with planned risk. Higher-timeframe confirmation checks whether the broader chart agrees with the shorter-term signal; disagreement can downgrade a signal to WAIT.
